The horizontal press: you push a loaded bar away from your chest while lying flat. It is the main chest builder in your week, and the lift your upper-body progression is measured on.

Start around 45 kg and let the app move it: hit 15 on every set → weight goes up next Monday.

How to do it
  1. Grip just outside shoulder width. Pull your shoulder blades down and back, and keep them pinned to the bench.
  2. Unrack and lower to the base of your sternum, elbows about 45° from your ribs, wrists stacked over them.
  3. Touch lightly, then press up and slightly back, so the bar finishes over your shoulders.
  4. Feet planted, ribs down. Stop the set when you have 2 clean reps left — that’s RPE 8.
Watch for
Elbows flared to 90° — moves load off the chest and onto the shoulder joint.
Bouncing the bar off your chest. That’s the hardest part of the rep; don’t skip it.

Stage 01

The split

onboarding/plan.tsx — replaces today’s ready.tsx

One question gets answered here: what am I on? The plan name is the headline, the three chips carry the constraints it was built from, and every training day is a card you can open. Nothing on this screen is a paragraph you have to read to continue.

Hierarchy, top to bottom

  • Plan name — structural, not a jammed string. Days / length / goal live in chips underneath.
  • Week strip — the shape of the commitment in one glance: four ink days, three rest.
  • Day cards — weekday, label, counts, and a neutral bar of sets-by-muscle with the lead muscle in ink. This is the shared DayCard that Today and Plan reuse.
  • Why this plan — a disclosure. It’s reassurance, not the point, and it stops the rationale being printed twice.
  • Targets — a one-line strip. Today owns calories and macros from now on, so they don’t need a card here.

Stage 02

A day

bottom sheet over the plan — not a route

A sheet, deliberately. The plan stays visible behind it, so opening three days in a row and then accepting never involves a back-stack. Height stops at 87% so the screen underneath is always legible as “where I came from”.

What it adds over the card

  • The real list, in program order, with sets × rep range and RPE — the thing the plan screen only counts.
  • A pattern tag per row (compound / secondary / isolation) so the ordering rule is visible, not just asserted.
  • Sets by muscle for this session, plus one line placing it in the week. Weekly volume vs MEV/MAV stays on the Plan tab — one owner per number.
  • Swap an exercise lives here, so substitution isn’t a mid-session-only power.
Sheets over routes is the whole reason the “go back and accept” step in your flow works: the accept button was never more than one swipe away.

Stage 03

An exercise

nested sheet — 92%, over the day sheet

Three questions, answered in a fixed order every time, so the screen is learnable: what is this, how do I do it, why is it here. Under 120 words of prose visible; everything else is a number, a chip, or a picture.

The six blocks, always in this order

  • Demo — a short loop, top of the sheet. When exercise.mediaUrl is null the tile is dropped entirely; no broken frame, the muscle map carries it.
  • One-line definition — what the movement is, in the user’s words. Never the dataset’s instruction blob.
  • Your prescription — sets / reps / RPE / rest as four tabular figures, plus the progression sentence. This is the app talking about you, so it sits above the generic content.
  • How to do it — four cues, max. Setup, the eccentric, the concentric, the stop condition.
  • Watch for — two mistakes, red. Red is the drive/intensity signal, and a form warning is the one place it earns a text colour.
  • What it works — muscle map, primary saturated, secondary at 45%. Reads faster than three labels ever will.
  • Why it’s in your plan — the generator’s reasoning in one sentence, tying the movement back to a rule and a number.

Stage 04

Accept

plan screen → completeOnboarding() → /today

Acceptance is one button on the plan screen, present from the first second — browsing days and exercises is optional depth, never a gate. Closing a sheet returns you to the plan with the day card you opened still in view, and the button still under your thumb.

Rules

  • The confirmation is a toast, not a screen. “Plan saved · first session Monday, Upper A”, then Today. A congratulations screen here is a wall between intent and action.
  • The button says what happens — “Use this plan”, not “Continue”.
  • The escape hatch is real: “Change my days or equipment” re-runs the generator with edited answers rather than making the user restart onboarding.
  • Account stays where it is, but never last: it sits before this screen, so the plan is the final thing you see before the app.

Stage 05

Today

(app)/home.tsx — first-run state

The landing screen answers what do I do right now, and on day one it has to re-introduce the plan the user just accepted rather than acting like it never happened.

First-run composition

  • Rings first — green outer for steps, red inner for active minutes, straight from Health Connect. This is the passive half of the app proving it’s alive before you’ve logged anything.
  • Plan strip — name plus the same week strip from stage 01, today marked. The plan gets re-introduced, once.
  • Today’s session — real rows (name + sets × reps), three shown, the rest collapsed into “+ 3 more”.
  • Energy card — the calorie and macro targets onboarding computed, with the check-in entry point. Until it exists, this card is where the promised numbers went.
  • One primary action, correct in both cases: training day → Start Upper A; rest day → First session Monday · preview it, which opens the day sheet. Never a dead end.
  • Streak reads 0 and that’s fine — day one is honest, not padded.
First-run styling clears the moment one session is logged; from then on Today is the normal screen.

What the field does

Five patterns worth stealing

Show structure before you ask for commitment

Fitbod asks about training experience, then shows a real first workout; equipment and finer goals come later. Our plan screen is fully browsable — days, exercises, cues — before “Use this plan” is ever tapped.

stormotion.io/blog/fitness-app-ux

Progressive disclosure, in stages

The consistent advice is to stop dumping the program as one long form or one long page. Plan → day → exercise gives three depths, and each one is a complete answer on its own.

screensdesign.com · onboarding teardowns

Under a minute from goal to “start”

Onboarding should reach plan-then-start fast. That’s why acceptance sits on the first screen of this flow and the detail sheets are optional, not sequential steps.

zfort.com · engagement & retention

Exercise detail is media + tags, not prose

Fitbod’s 900-move library is the benchmark: a demo, muscle tags, a difficulty rating. We add the one thing a generated plan can say that a library can’t — why this movement is in your week.

gymnoteplus.com · Fitbod vs Hevy

Keep the session screen out of it

Everyone agrees the in-workout screen stays distraction-free. So the sheets stop at the exercise detail; the how-to lives here, before the session, not on top of the scroll-dial.

madappgang.com · typical mistakes

Explain the plan, don’t just assert it

Coach-built apps (Ladder) sell the reasoning; generator apps often hide it. Ours is rule-based and auditable, so every screen can name the rule that produced it — a real advantage, used sparingly.

outdoorsynomad.com · Ladder review

Held to

The system, unchanged

  • Every surface is glass over red/green ambient blobs; only buttons, selected cards and the ink center band stay opaque.
  • Ink is the only emphasis colour. Red appears on active minutes and form warnings; green on “saved”, goal progress and confirm actions. Nothing else is coloured.
  • Sets-by-muscle bars are neutral with the lead muscle in ink — volume isn’t drive or progress, so it doesn’t get a hue.
  • Inter throughout, tabular figures on every prescription and target.
  • Dark mode is the archived charcoal palette, switched at token level. Try the toggle up top.

Not built here

Left out on purpose

  • Swap-exercise flow. The entry points are placed; the picker is the library screen, which already exists and just needs rerouting under Plan.
  • Lower A / Upper B / Lower B sheets. One day is the component; the other three are data.
  • Drag-to-dismiss physics. Specced as a gesture, prototyped as a tap — it’s a native concern, not a layout one.
  • The rest-day variant of Today. Described in stage 05; same screen, different primary action.
